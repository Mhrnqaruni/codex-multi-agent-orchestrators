Why Stripe beats PayPal for your use case

Between just PayPal and Stripe, I would choose Stripe.

Reason:
- Stripe is much stronger for subscription-style software products. Its official stack already includes hosted [Checkout](https://stripe.com/gb/checkout), [Payment Links](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/no-code), [Billing](https://stripe.com/gb/billing/pricing), and a [customer portal](https://docs.stripe.com/no-code/customer-portal).
- Stripe is better suited to “user logs in, backend knows subscription state, app unlocks premium features” flows. Their docs are very explicit about using [webhooks for subscription status](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/webhooks).
- PayPal does support subscriptions and webhooks, and recurring payments are built into [PayPal Checkout](https://www.paypal.com/uk/business/accept-payments/recurring-payments), so it is not a bad product. But its pricing and product surface are more fragmented for a small SaaS/app team. This is an inference from the official docs and fee pages, not a direct PayPal admission.

Pricing direction

Stripe:
- [Stripe Checkout UK pricing](https://stripe.com/gb/checkout): `1.5% + 20p` for standard UK cards.
- [Stripe Billing](https://stripe.com/gb/billing/pricing): `0.7%` of billing volume for recurring billing.
- [Stripe Tax Basic](https://stripe.com/tax/pricing): `0.5%` per taxed transaction for no-code Billing/Checkout flows.

PayPal:
- [PayPal UK fees](https://www.paypal.com/gb/business/paypal-business-fees) list `2.9% + fixed fee` for standard domestic commercial transactions, while other card-processing products have separate pricing.
- [PayPal recurring payments](https://www.paypal.com/uk/business/accept-payments/recurring-payments) are built in, but the overall setup is less clean for a subscription app business.

So for a small UK software company, Stripe is usually the easier default.

The most important thing you must not miss

Your real constraint is not PayPal vs Stripe.
It is mobile platform policy.

For iPhone:
- Apple’s terms say subscription services purchased within the app must use In-App Purchase.
- Apple’s [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) can reduce commission to `15%` if you qualify.
- Apple says [90% of submissions are reviewed in under 24 hours](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/), but approval is still not guaranteed.

For Android:
- Google Play says [digital products/content in Android apps use Google Play billing](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing).
- Google’s [service fee page](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en) says automatically renewing subscriptions are `15%`.
- Google also says reviews can take [a few hours up to 7 days or longer in exceptional cases](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859654?hl=en).

For Microsoft Store:
- If you distribute through Microsoft Store and sell digital items used in the app, Microsoft policy says you must use the [Microsoft Store in-product purchase API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/publish/store-policy-archive/store-policy-7-13).
- If you distribute your Windows app directly from your own website, you can use Stripe normally.

This means:
A single “just use Stripe/PayPal everywhere” plan is not the best architecture for a normal consumer premium app.

Best architecture for your business

Use this model:

Your backend decides:

- `premium_expired`

3. Payment sources by channel
- Web: Stripe Checkout or Payment Links
- Direct Windows app: Stripe Checkout opened in browser/webview, then login reflects entitlement
- iOS: App Store In-App Purchase
- Android: Google Play Billing

4. Webhooks / server notifications
Your backend updates entitlement on:
- successful purchase,
- renewal,
- failed payment,
- cancellation,
- expiry.

5. App login check
Every app asks your backend: “is this user premium?”
Do not trust the device alone.

Use RevenueCat if you want this done quickly and cleanly.

- RevenueCat also supports [Stripe web payments](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/integrations/stripe).
- Their customer data model explicitly tracks entitlements unlocked by App Store, Play Store, or Stripe.
- Their homepage says pricing starts after `$2,500` in monthly tracked revenue ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/)).

For your size and timeline, this is probably the highest-leverage tool in the stack.
